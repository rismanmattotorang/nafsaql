\section{Phase 25: An In-Process Index, a Graph without a Model, and a 256-Question Sweep}
\label{sec:phase25}

This phase assessed the Knowledge module as merged, read the 2024 to 2026 literature on building and searching vector and graph indexes, and rebuilt the search path
(10 November 2026; shared host, load average 6 to 8 for the measurements below and 24 to 129 for the baseline). Records:
\texttt{docs/research/knowledge\_sota\_2026.md}, \texttt{docs/spec/knowledge\_sota\_spec.md} (FR-K67 to FR-K82, NFR-K13 to NFR-K16, UX-1 to UX-21) and
\texttt{docs/decisions/018-knowledge-index-phase25.md}. No public benchmark and no model weights were downloaded, so nothing here is claimed against published
systems; the claims are measured changes against the module of Phase~24.

\subsection{Assessment}

Every search made several round trips to the store, and under load each cost seconds: on a 443-passage base at load 24 to 129 a search by words took 1.2\,s at
the median and a hybrid search 5.3\,s. The graph cost a model call per passage (17 to 28\,s), so most bases never had one, and its fixed two-hop walk answered 15 of
20 questions against 18 for plain retrieval. A re-read embedded every passage again. Reworded questions were the weak point (mean reciprocal rank 0.45 against 0.91
for quoted ones), and the 20-question set could not separate two settings.

\subsection{What the literature says, and what was taken from it}

An in-process inverted index answers in about 2\,ms where a search server takes 50 (BM25S); below a few hundred thousand passages an exact scan of the vectors is
both the fastest and the most accurate index (measured here: 100{,}000 $\times$ 1{,}024 in 20.8\,ms). The gain of graph retrieval comes from Personalized PageRank
over a passage-and-entity graph, not from typed relations: HippoRAG~2 loses 6.1 points of recall at 5 without passage nodes and 0.7 without its model filter, and
LinearRAG and KET-RAG build such graphs with no model call. Fixed-size chunks beat semantic chunking on real documents; a convex combination of normalised scores
beats reciprocal-rank fusion on nine BEIR sets. Not taken: query-time model expansion, GraphRAG's query modes and community summaries (39{,}000 to 331{,}000
prompt tokens a question), an approximate-nearest-neighbour library, a full search engine, and anything that needs PDFium, ONNX Runtime or unapproved weights.

\subsection{Built}

\paragraph{An in-process index (FR-K67 to FR-K69).} SurrealDB remains the source of truth. The service holds, per base and per document, the passages, an
inverted index over their words, their vectors and the terms they name, within a memory limit (512\,MB by default; a base that does not fit keeps the store path).
The words are scored by BM25F over four fields (title, heading path, context line, body) with the store's analyzer (lowercase, ASCII folding, Snowball stems) and one
addition: an identifier is a term of its own beside its parts, so \texttt{vector\_weight} or \texttt{Qwen3-1.7B} is not cut into words that match everywhere. A
change touches one document's part; a base is immutable once made, so a search in flight keeps a consistent view; a generation counter keeps a load from installing
rows read before a change; and every 30\,s, off the search path, the index's counts are compared with the store's and the documents that differ are read again.

\paragraph{A graph with no model call (FR-K71, FR-K72).} Terms are read from the text by rule: acronyms, identifiers, runs of capitalised words, and single
capitalised words that are also capitalised away from the start of a sentence. A term named by at least two passages (and at most a fifth of the base) is a node,
linked to the passages that name it with a weight that grows with its rarity. For the 847-passage base this gives 1{,}306 terms and 5{,}975 links in under a
millisecond. Personalized PageRank over this bipartite graph (20 rounds of power iteration, seeded by the best passages of the search and the terms the question
names) is available as a third ranking. The model-built graph of Phases~20 to~22 stays as the layer of typed, quoted relations that a person reads.

\paragraph{Embedding once (FR-K73, FR-K79a).} A re-read keeps the vector of every passage whose embedded text did not change. The vector of a question is
remembered. And once the index had left the question's embedding as 99.9\,\% of a hybrid search, the embedder was changed to keep the cache rows of the tokens a
text shares with the previous one: every question starts with the same instruction. Attention is causal, so the kept rows are what would be computed again; the
vectors are identical (cosine 1.000000 on 30 questions).

\paragraph{An evaluation that can separate two settings (FR-K75).} Three question sets over the 847-passage base, each accepted phrase verbatim in at most three
passages and checked by script: 60 quoted questions, 146 reworded ones (on average 9\,\% of a question's content words occur in the answering passage) and 50
questions that need two passages. A harness reads the base once, embeds each question once and ranks every question under every setting in milliseconds.

\subsection{What the sweep decided, including what it rejected}

Fifty-nine settings were ranked on the three sets. \textbf{Adopted:} length normalisation $b=0.5$ (the answer first for 53 of 60 quoted questions against 50 at
the textbook 0.75). \textbf{Confirmed:} reciprocal-rank fusion with the vectors at 0.1 (Phase~23's choice, now on a set seven times larger). \textbf{Measured and
not adopted:} (i) the convex combination, better on quoted and two-passage questions and worse on reworded ones, equal pooled (0.387 against 0.388); (ii) BM25F
field weights from 0 to 3, each within one question of flat weights on every set; (iii) feedback terms from the first passages, which cost eight quoted questions
their first place; (iv) the PageRank arm in the default search, which leaves the first eight passages unchanged on every set and raises recall at 20 on reworded
questions from 0.329 to as much as 0.377. The arm serves the \texttt{graph} mode and waits for a reranker to use what it finds below rank eight. An earlier run at
$b=0.75$ had shown the arm raising two-passage recall; at $b=0.5$ the plain search finds those passages itself, so the gain had been the baseline's weakness.

\subsection{The readers}

No public score exists for the PDF reader's reading order or for the TeX stripper, so the same document was read both ways (FR-K74): of this report read from its
34 TeX files, 98.0\,\% of the numbers and 91.6\,\% of the distinct words are in the reading of its PDF. The missing words are macro and label names that the TeX
stripper lets through inside mathematics and tables, recorded as an open defect.

\subsection{Interface}

An audit of the web interface ranked 21 defects. Fixed: an answer in flight survives leaving the Chat page; a streaming answer is redrawn at most every 150\,ms;
\texttt{[n]} in an answer opens its source with a preview of the passage; an upload has a row per file with its stage, a cancel and errors that stay; the documents
table is updated row by row; a search result opens its passage in context; the graph can be zoomed, panned and filtered and says how much of itself it shows; route
changes move focus and are announced; errors persist and no status code reaches a person; expensive actions ask first. Not done: the Rooms access key still
travels in the WebSocket address (a protocol change), and three items need server routes that do not exist yet.

\subsection{What this does not establish}

Retrieval is faster by the factors of Table~\ref{tab:p25time}; it is not measurably more accurate. The reworded question remains open: the answering passage is
first for 12\,\% of such questions and within the first eight for 24\,\%, and the 0.6B embedding model alone ranks it first for 6\,\%. The remedies the literature
offers (a reranker or a stronger embedder, questions written for each passage when it is indexed) need model weights or hours of the quick model, and a comparison
with published systems needs public corpora; none was downloaded without the owner's approval.
