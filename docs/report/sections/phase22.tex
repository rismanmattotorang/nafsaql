\section{Phase 22: The Gate and the Harness Against Two Open Projects}
\label{sec:phase22}

Phase~22 (8 November 2026) reads vLLM Semantic Router~\cite{semanticrouter2026} and DeepSeek Harness~\cite{deepseekharness2026} in full against the
Aql gate of Section~\ref{sec:math} and the agent harness of Sections~\ref{sec:harness2} and~\ref{sec:phase16}, closes the gaps the reading exposes, and records what it did not copy and
why. The research note is \texttt{docs/research/router\_harness\_comparison.md}, the specification
\texttt{docs/spec/router\_harness\_upgrade\_spec.md}, and the decision \texttt{docs/decisions/015-router-harness-phase22.md}. The ground rule of every phase
holds: nothing here adds a model call to a request. Both projects measured their own gates at 79\,ms to 9.8\,s per request on a CPU; ours stays at the
0.08\,s it costs today.

\subsection{Where the design was already ahead}

Semantic Router is a decision layer in front of many models: signals (keyword, embedding, domain, complexity, reask, and typed questions answered by a
``decision model''), Boolean rules over the signals that pick a decision, and an algorithm inside the decision that picks the model. Its decision rule is
lexicographic and hand-tuned (tier, then priority, then evidence; a \texttt{multi\_factor} min--max utility with 0.25 weights); the paper's
$\arg\max\,\mathrm{Quality}-\lambda\cdot\mathrm{Cost}$ is a framing that no selector implements. It has no exact verifier in production: of seven declared
verifier kinds three exist, two of which never approve, and the reference AutoMix verifier returns a uniform random number when \texttt{transformers} is
missing. Its cascade stops at the first response over a threshold and, when none passes, serves the \emph{last} response rather than the best. Our gate is
one rule derived from expected loss, $L(\hat e_1-\hat e_2)>\lambda\,\Delta c$, with $\lambda$ a price of latency in real units; our verifiers' verdicts are
final; when nothing passes, the best candidate is returned, or the system abstains.

DeepSeek Harness (\texttt{dsh}) is a ReAct loop over an event-sourced session log: every model request is derived from the log, so any prompt the model
saw can be rebuilt byte for byte. It has no verifiers, no search over candidates, no rollback, no compile gate, no grammar-constrained decoding and no
maximum number of steps per turn; a run ends when the model says it is complete, and its benchmarks measure host overhead with no model. Its sandbox
binds the filesystem read-only but does not unshare the network. Ours ends a run only when an exact verifier accepts or the budget is spent, keeps a
best-first tree of candidates, checks a compile before the verifier, rolls back, stops on the fourth identical failure, and denies the network.

\subsection{What was genuinely better there, and was built}

Each item is a pure mechanism, each tested, none spending a model call.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Certified per-bin risk} (FR-R1). Semantic Router's System~One cascade accepts a cheap answer only in a score bin whose one-sided
  Clopper--Pearson upper bound on the error rate, at $\alpha/B$ over $B$ pre-declared bins, is at or below a configured risk. Our calibrated mode was
  promoted on held-out ECE $\le 0.05$, which bounds the \emph{average} miscalibration; the per-bin bound is the stronger guarantee, and our exact verifiers
  supply the error counts for free. The fitted calibration now keeps five equal-mass bins of held-out $\hat e_1$ with their counts and, for each, the
  smallest $u$ with $I_u(k{+}1,\,n{-}k)\ge 1-\alpha/B$ by bisection on the regularised incomplete beta ($u=1-(\alpha/B)^{1/n}$ when $k=0$). A draft is kept
  only in a certified bin; otherwise the decision says \texttt{uncertified\_bin} with the bound, and a calibration with no certified bin is not promoted.
  With $\alpha=0.05$ and five bins, a bin of twenty drafts none of which was wrong still bounds its error at 0.21, so a young ledger certifies little and
  escalates more; the bound tightens with the ledger by itself.
\item \textbf{A declared unknown} (FR-R2). A draft without log-probabilities (a backend that does not return them, a failed sample) used to look like a
  confident draft. The gate now applies a declared policy, \texttt{on\_unknown} = \texttt{escalate} (default), \texttt{keep} or \texttt{fixed}, and the
  decision's rule says which (\texttt{unknown\_escalate}) and which feature was missing, after Semantic Router's three-valued rule logic.
\item \textbf{Reask} (FR-R3). A user turn whose content words overlap the previous user turn's at Jaccard $\ge 0.6$ is a reask: asking again is evidence
  that the last answer failed. A reask of an unverified draft escalates under the fixed rule, and the feature is logged so a refit can learn its weight.
  Lexical rather than cosine, because the gate runs without the embedder and must stay at its cost.
\item \textbf{A polarity guard} (FR-R4). A retrieval-memory hit whose stored question differs from the query by at most two content words with a negation
  cue or an antonym pair on one side only is dropped: ``is caching enabled'' no longer retrieves the stored answer to ``is caching disabled''.
\item \textbf{An anchored token meter} (FR-H1). The harness transcript's size was bytes/4; it is now the server's reported \texttt{prompt\_tokens} of the
  last call plus bytes/3 of what was appended since, cleared by a prune or a compaction. On a CPU where prefill is the cost, the difference between an
  estimate and the server's count is the difference between compacting at the right step and the wrong one.
\item \textbf{Prune, then compact} (FR-H2). Compaction was one step into a fresh task message, so a long test log took the whole transcript down with it.
  Now older observations over 1,200 bytes are cut to a 600-byte head and a 200-byte tail (never the task, never the newest exchange, never an action apart
  from its observation), and only if the transcript is still over its budget is it compacted. Each step records whether it was pruned.
\item \textbf{No edit from a cut reply} (FR-H3). A patch or whole file in a reply that ended on the token budget is refused with an instruction to send a
  shorter edit; a \texttt{run} is still read. \texttt{dsh} drops every tool call from such a message.
\item \textbf{The repeat notice} (FR-H4). The same action text sent a third time draws a gentle notice, a fifth a detailed one naming it. The stall guard
  reacts to the same \emph{failure}; this names the same \emph{attempt} before it fails. The notice is appended after the guard has taken the step's
  signature: the first version appended it before, and an existing test (a missing file run again and again) failed because the altered observation hid
  the identical failure from the guard.
\item \textbf{Two-lane ingestion} (FR-K61). Found by this phase's live sweep: a 187-byte upload waited five minutes behind a 517-passage re-index, and the
  graph build behind both. Parsing, chunking and storing now run on a fast lane that sets the document ready, and vectors and the graph queue on a slow
  lane, one job at a time.
\end{itemize}

Specified and deferred, with the reason: a difficulty prior from contrastive prototype banks (FR-R5; a labelled example set per domain and a measured gain
first, since a new feature invalidates stored calibrations), and spilling a long observation to a file with a head-and-tail preview (FR-H5; the action
vocabulary has no \emph{read}, so this is a prompt change to measure, not a mechanism).

\subsection{What was not copied}

A model of 0.3\,B parameters or more as the per-request gate, which on four cores costs more than the small model's answer; model-judged cascades
(self-rated confidence, a judge-written fusion, a random-fallback verifier, serving the last response rather than the best), none with a measured gain in
the repository; hand-weighted utilities with no price of latency; model-claimed completion and unbounded loops, strictly weaker than verifiers and budgets;
a plugin architecture two of whose four postmortems are composition bugs (a YAML expression in a \texttt{disabled:} field that silently never evaluated, so
the filesystem tools were off and the snapshot recorded the failure as expected output); and a session log that uploads up to 8\,MiB per request to the
provider. Nothing leaves this machine.

\subsection{Validation}

The workspace has 436 tests, all passing in release mode, with \texttt{rustfmt}, \texttt{clippy -D warnings} and \texttt{cargo deny} clean. Each
mechanism has a unit test: the Clopper--Pearson bound against known values (0 of 20 at 0.1391, 1 of 10 at 0.3942, 5 of 100 at 0.1023, from
\texttt{scipy}), bins that certify only the safe ones, the reask and polarity predicates, the unknown policies, the prune keeping the newest exchange, the
refusal of an edit from a cut reply, and the repeat notice's order against the stall guard. Running the full suite found three defects of ours, all
fixed: a wrong reference value in our own test of the bound (the one-sided 5/100 bound is 0.1023, not the two-sided 0.0975); the certified-bin rule
overriding budget mode, which fixes the escalation rate by design and escalated every draft (it now applies to the calibrated rule only); and a
round-trip test and a sweep that assumed vectors exist when a document is ready, which two lanes make untrue. The live sweep on the final binary, on a
host at load 6--17: contract tests, security 26 of 26, Rooms 10 of 10, end to end 67 of 67, the knowledge sweep 78 of 78 with chat and the PDF and
ontology checks, harness 55 of 55 and acceptance 9 of 9. Not measured, and said so: the gate's certified bins on a live ledger (the reference
machine's ledger is too young to certify a bin at $\alpha=0.05$, so calibrated mode stays unpromoted and the fixed rule decides), and the harness's prune
against a long agent run (a unit test shows it cuts and keeps the newest exchange; a paired run on task success is future work).
