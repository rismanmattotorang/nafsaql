\section{Phase 23: Three Open Measurements, a Hardening Review, and Retrieval at Scale}
\label{sec:phase23}

Phase~22 left three things unmeasured and the knowledge track's last phase unwritten. This section reports them (8 November 2026, shared host, load average
6 to 130, so every time is a pessimistic bound). The records are \texttt{docs/decisions/016-knowledge-hardening-phase8.md},
\texttt{docs/roadmap/machine\_bound\_plan.md} and the specification \texttt{docs/spec/knowledge\_base\_spec.md} (v1.9).

\subsection{Certified gate bins on a live ledger}

The ledger held 29 labelled drafts, too few for any bin. A \emph{probe} was added: a forced-fast draft that still draws its agreement samples
(\texttt{gate\_probe}), so a labelled gate sample costs one draft and no System~2 call, with an evaluation system \texttt{fast\_probe} around it. Two hundred
and four GSM8K and MBPP training items gave 203 labelled drafts (190 mathematics, 13 code), 51 of them wrong (25.1\,\%). A report
(\texttt{nafs-eval} example \texttt{gate\_report}) fits the calibration, bins the held-out estimate and checks the certificate out of sample over 400 random 60/40
splits.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The fit is well calibrated} (held-out ECE 0.024, Brier 0.117) but \textbf{cannot be promoted}: NFR-020 asks for 2,900 held-out samples.
\item \textbf{The certificate holds.} With two bins of about 100 drafts the lower bin has 8 wrong of 101, so its bound is 0.150 and nothing is certified at the
  default $\texttt{max\_risk}=0.15$ yet. Among about 1,300 held-out bin tests at $\texttt{max\_risk}$ 0.15 to 0.40, one broke its bound significantly (a
  binomial tail of 0.01 or less). A first reading that compared observed rates with the bounds directly counted 5 to 12\,\% of bins as violations; that
  comparison flags a bin of ten drafts for one unlucky error, and the tail test is the correct one.
\item \textbf{At this size it costs coverage.} On the same held-out drafts the fixed rule keeps 78.2\,\% at 10.1\,\% wrong; the calibrated rule on expected loss alone
  keeps 85.8\,\% at 16.6\,\% wrong; with certified bins it keeps 63.6\,\% at 9.5\,\% (\texttt{max\_risk} 0.40), 54.5\,\% at 9.9\,\% (0.25) and 5.4\,\% at 12.4\,\% (0.15).
  Bounds of a hundred drafts are too wide to beat a rule that uses the samples' agreement. The number of bins was made to follow the sample size (about one per
  hundred drafts, two to five), which moved the 0.25 row from 17\,\% to 55\,\% of drafts kept.
\end{itemize}

The fixed rule therefore stays in force. A \emph{label stream} (\texttt{[labels]}, off by default) fills the ledger: while no user request is in flight it
answers verifiable tasks with the quick model alone and lets the exact verifier judge each draft; a user arriving cancels the draft in progress. At 90 an hour
the floor of 2,900 is about 32 hours of idle time.

\subsection{The harness's prune, measured}

Eight tasks whose failures print an element-by-element comparison were run twice per arm, prune against compact-at-once with compaction forced at 1,400
estimated tokens. The model is never shown more than about 930 bytes of the verifier's evidence, so at the default threshold (1,200 bytes) the prune fired in
none of 16 runs, and at 600 bytes in 2 of 16. Eight of 16 runs were solved in each arm in both pairs, with identical action sequences throughout. It stays on as
insurance against a long \texttt{run} output; no gain is claimed (Section~\ref{sec:phase22}).

\subsection{Memory and hybrid, measured on an epoch-1 module}

Memorilla was trained on the 66-passage sections base (234 examples; validation loss 8.77 to 1.46 after one epoch; the second epoch was abandoned). On the 20
paper questions: closed-book 0 of 20, \texttt{rag} 18 of 20, \texttt{hybrid} 17 of 20, \texttt{memory} alone 0 of 20, with degenerate text (\texttt{0000\ldots},
\texttt{None None\ldots}) where closed-book answers fluently. In the training decoder in f32 the claim accuracy with the module is 0.55 and without it 0.55: the
module read no knowledge, as the paper predicts for a module with no pre-training. The memory arms are therefore a negative result at this scale, and the
machine-bound plan makes the GPU pre-train the next step for them.

\subsection{The hardening review}

Eight gaps were found and closed (SR-K11 to SR-K13): a file was buffered whole before the size limit was applied and a request could carry a gigabyte; the zip
budget summed header sizes that can lie; a web page's \texttt{javascript:} link survived into stored text; nothing bounded the queue, the documents in a base, the
passages one file makes or a parser's running time; deleting a base left its training run going; the memory reader kept the deleted base's prepared documents; the
ledger kept the passages every reply was answered from (93 replies on the reference machine); and training runs were unlimited across bases. A live security sweep
on its own gateway with small limits passes 36 of 36 checks, including that deleting a document, then its base, removes the originals, the passages and the passages
copied into stored replies. Two further defects came out of the same work: a vector write that met a transient transaction conflict failed its document, and the
JSON schemas of Phase~22 omitted the three new gate rules, which a validating client would have rejected.

\subsection{Retrieval}

Ranking where the answering passage lands (\texttt{scripts/kb\_retrieval\_rank.py}) separates retrieval from generation, and 70 questions over two bases of the
paper (50 that quote the passages, 20 heavily paraphrased) make small changes visible.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A strong word match is kept.} Vector search alone left 4 of 20 answering passages outside its top 20; one, ranked first by the words, was dropped by the
  chat similarity test, so the question went unanswered. A passage whose BM25 score reaches 10.0 is now kept (off-topic questions scored at most 8.0, answering passages
  at least 10.8).
\item \textbf{Function words are left out of a words search.} Lexical MRR on the whole-paper base rose from 0.903 to 0.958 and, at 50,000 passages, one very common
  word in the query had cost 18\,s against 1.4\,s for the rare word alone.
\item \textbf{The meaning arm is weighted 0.1.} With equal weights the fused list was \emph{worse} than the words alone (MRR 0.885 and 0.854 against 0.913 and 0.895);
  pooled over the 70 questions, weight 0.1 gave MRR 0.844 and 0.779 against 0.800 and 0.748, and kept the meaning arm's recall.
\item \textbf{Measured and not adopted:} a model-written hypothetical passage as query expansion (8\,s a question; it cost 0.03 MRR on the quoted questions and gave
  a mixed result on the paraphrased ones), near-duplicate removal (the answer is in the top four for 57 of 70 questions at every threshold), AND-first search (it
  applies to one question in 70), and a reranker (the answer is in the hybrid top four for 19 of 20 questions).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Passage size.} At the same 4,800 characters of prompt, passages of 600, 1,200 and 2,400 characters (8, 4 and 2 slots) put the answer in the prompt for 94, 94
and 88\,\% of the 49 quoted questions and for 70, 70 and 50\,\% of the 20 paraphrased ones; the passage is first for 84, 90 and 84\,\% of the quoted questions. The
shipped 1,200 characters ties for first on recall and is best on rank, so it stays (\texttt{scripts/kb\_chunk\_size\_experiment.py},
\texttt{benchmarks/kb\_chunk\_size.json}; one base of six documents).

\subsection{Scale: 1,000 documents, 50,000 passages}

A synthetic base of 1,000 documents and 50,000 passages (skewed vocabulary, random unit vectors of 1,024 dimensions, built straight in the store;
\texttt{rust/crates/nafs-kb/tests/scale\_it.rs}, \texttt{benchmarks/kb\_scale.json}) was timed on the shared host (load average 20 to 130). The
steady-state numbers: listing the bases 0.15\,s, listing the 1,000 documents 0.28\,s, opening a document 0.37\,s, counting the base's vectors 0.06\,s, a
search by meaning through the store's HNSW index 0.37\,s at the median (0.93\,s at the 95th percentile). The scale test found five defects, each fixed:

\begin{itemize}
\item Counting a base's vectors scanned every embedding (200\,MB) and every search asked: 181\,s under load. It now reads the documents' own counts, 0.06\,s.
\item A words query is OR-ed and scored, so a word in every passage made the store score all 50,000: 18\,s to over 30\,s, and a client that gave up did not stop the
  store, whose abandoned queries then starved the next ones. Function words are dropped; in bases of 4,000 passages or more the words that fill a capped probe (a fiftieth
  of the base) are dropped too when rarer ones remain; the store stops a query itself at 10\,s (\texttt{TIMEOUT}), and a hybrid search then answers by meaning. A rare
  word plus the commonest word now takes 2.2\,s at the median (7 of 10 had been stopped), a rare word plus a word in every title line 3.1\,s (5 of 5 had been
  stopped); a query of common words alone is still stopped. A rare word alone takes 2 to 6\,s under load.
\item A vector write that met a transient transaction conflict failed its document; it is retried.
\item Deleting a base ran one statement over 50,000 passages and passed the client's 60\,s limit, leaving it half deleted. Passages now go in batches of 500 in the
  background (a base leaves lists and searches at once, a restart resumes an interrupted deletion), at about 16 passages a second, under an hour for 50,000: removing a
  passage from the HNSW index is the cost.
\item A first reading put the HNSW query at 26 to 110\,s and led to an in-memory copy of the vectors. The reading was a cold start and queries made beside abandoned
  heavy ones, and was withdrawn; the warm index is usable. The copy (exact search over a normalised array, 18 to 22\,ms, 100 of 100 planted vectors first, after a
  164 to 296\,s load) is kept as an option (\texttt{vector\_cache\_mb}, off by default).
\end{itemize}

NFR-K07 is therefore met for listing, counting and search by meaning; search by words is usable but slow under load.

\subsection{Validation}

The workspace has 450 tests, all passing in release mode, with \texttt{rustfmt}, \texttt{clippy -D warnings} and \texttt{cargo deny} clean. The live sweep on the final
binary (host load 15 to 50): contract tests passed, security 26 of 26, Rooms 10 of 10, end to end 68 of 68, the knowledge sweep 79 of 79 with chat on the paper base,
the knowledge security sweep 36 of 36 with chat, harness 55 of 55 and acceptance 9 of 9. Not measured: the calibrated gate once the label stream has filled the ledger
(it has begun), a scale run on a quiet host, and the memory arms beyond one epoch.

\subsection{What this machine cannot do}

Four things are blocked by the host, not by code, and each has a plan (\texttt{docs/roadmap/machine\_bound\_plan.md}): the gate's 2,900-sample floor (the label
stream, no new hardware), release-scale evaluation (a quiet host or ten nights of slices), Memorilla's general reading ability (about ten GPU-hours of pre-training),
and the speed studies that need an idle host.
