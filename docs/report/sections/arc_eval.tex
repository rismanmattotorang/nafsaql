\subsection{Does the reasoning cache answer the same problem, and only it?}
\label{sec:eval:arc}
Twelve grade-school and competition-style problems (change from a purchase,
divisor counts, distance, simple interest, perimeter, sums of odd numbers,
work rate, discount, a removed number, committees, least common multiples,
reading time) each come with an oracle that gives the right answer to any
values. System~2 answered each with the cache off and the oracle's answer as
reference, then built an entry from its own reply. Sixty-four variants test
the entries: 14 in other words with the same numbers, 24 with the same words
and other numbers, 12 with both, and 14 \emph{near misses} on the same topic,
usually with the same numbers, that need other reasoning (the area where the
perimeter was kept, the even numbers where the odd ones were, an extra
2-dollar eraser). Each question went to the cache alone, with no fall-back,
and a hit is right when its answer equals the oracle's.

\emph{Building.} All twelve entries were built, seven in one round of the
builder and five in two; every first round that failed was refused by a check
(an ambiguous input description, a missing root, a function without
narration, and once a program that returned 2 for a least common multiple of
36). A build took 461\,s on average, 7.2 times the 64\,s of the answer it was
built from; CPU-ARC reports 12 to 84 times in dollars with a frontier builder.

\emph{Matching} (Table~\ref{tab:arc}). Every question with the stored wording
and other numbers was matched by its template and answered right in a median
of 95\,ms, with no model. Thirteen of fourteen rewordings were answered right;
the one miss writes ``Five numbers'' in words, which the value guard refuses
by design. Only four of twelve questions changed in both words and numbers
were recognised: the rest score below a meaning similarity of 0.80. The first
run exposed the weak point of the meaning tier: it finds the same \emph{topic},
and six of fourteen near misses were answered, all wrongly (the sum of
divisors as their number, compound interest as simple), although System~1's
extraction was asked to say whether the question was the same. Asked directly
whether two questions ask for the same quantity, System~1 judged 21 of 23
meaning-tier matches right; with that question and the contrast words, the
rerun answered no near miss, and the 53 answers it gave were all right. The
contrast-word list was written while looking at these items, so its share of
that result is optimistic; the direct question was measured unchanged. With
the checks, a floor of 0.80 lost nothing; 0.85 is the default.

\emph{Time.} Through the chat endpoint, with the entry trusted and the arms
interleaved, a question with other numbers was answered from the cache in
0.1\,s, against 20.8\,s on the usual path and 85.5\,s from
System~2 (medians of six questions each). A reworded one took 6.9 to 10.9\,s from the
cache (three short System~1 calls), against medians of 18.1\,s on the usual
path and 81.9\,s from System~2; on two of six, System~1 judged the rewording a
different question and the request went to the models, which answered it.
Every answer in every arm was right. With these times the cost of a build is
repaid after about six re-numbered questions that would otherwise have
gone to System~2, or about 23 that the usual path would have answered.

\emph{Compilation.} The twelve entries compile into one standard-library file of 28\,KB. Run as a new process on every question, it answered the 36 that match a stored template, all right, in a median of 78\,ms with no model, and refused the other 40; on all 76 its answer agreed with the runtime's. Two faults of the compiler were found on built entries and fixed with tests: inputs a callee reads from its caller's scope were not passed on (found while developing it, on the first entry), and a local variable named like a function was renamed with it (found by the module's own check, which left that entry out with the reason instead of answering wrongly).
