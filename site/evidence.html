Read this first

All numbers come from one Intel Core i3-8100 (4 cores, AVX2, 32 GB, no GPU) that was shared with other workloads during most runs, with tens of items per comparison. They show direction, not release-grade statistics. Absolute speeds are lower bounds. The project's own release gates ask for 700 to 6,200 paired items, which this machine cannot produce.

Method

How a number gets onto this page.

Interleaved A/B

The two arms of a comparison alternate inside one run, so a change in the machine's load hits both. A sequential run once showed a 30 % gain that vanished when the runs were interleaved.

Paired rounds

Where the quantity allows it, short paired rounds are compared one against one, and the count is reported: "12 of 12 paired rounds", "faster in 10 of 12 pairs".

A shared machine, labelled

The reference machine runs other work. Runs record the load average, and results are marked "shared machine". A gain has to survive the noise to be adopted.

Exact checkers decide "right"

Where a task has a checker, an answer counts as right only when the checker passes it. The checkers' own error rates are measured and published below.

Stored runs

Each figure comes from a run kept in benchmarks/ or a document in the repository. The table below links each one.

Negative results stay

A change becomes a default only on a measured gain. What was built and did not pay is kept in the repository, switched off, with its measurement.

What these numbers do not show

  • The between-turns gain needs the pause to be longer than the reading: 3 to 5 s for the quick model, 7 to 17 s for the reasoning model on a shared machine. One reasoning turn of six arrived early and waited 18.6 s, as before.
  • A fourth writing thread was not compared with three head to head after the change. The default stays three threads to write and four to read.
  • In the gateway's real traffic most reuses are short copies, not takes (216 against 19 on the quick model after a morning of test traffic). The take matters for the long transcript.
  • Taking work from the front of a neighbour's share was 5 % slower writing and 10 % slower reading than fixed shares on three threads. Taking from the back removed the loss.

Source: FreeToken assessment, section 5; decisions 026 and 027.

Open and not done

What the evidence does not cover yet.

  • Reworded questions. A question worded differently from the document finds its passage first 12 % of the time and in the first eight 24 % of the time. The remedies need larger models than this release ships.
  • Release-scale evaluation. Not done. It needs a faster machine.
  • The learned gate. It waits for its ledger: 1,294 of the 2,900 checked answers it asks for.
  • Distillation and memory tokens. The first distillation cycle and the pre-training of the memory-token module need a faster machine.
  • Formal proofs. The Lean checker is exact, but the reasoning model cannot yet write valid Lean 4, so Lean tasks end in a labelled abstention.
  • Recall over a long history. The memory passes its live checks; its quality over a long history has not been measured.
  • No public benchmark was run for parsing or retrieval. No "state of the art" claim is made. What is claimed is each measured change against the setting it replaced.
  • Not done in 0.2.0: more diagram kinds, a search-and-replace edit format for long files, versions of a regenerated answer, scanned PDFs and pictures, server-side settings per person.
