Use cases

Sixteen places where the work cannot leave the building.

Each case names who it is for, why it has to be local, what in Nafs-Aql serves it, the evidence that the need is real, and the limit to know before you start.

  • Regulated professions
  • Public sector, education and research
  • Industry and engineering
  • Constrained environments

How to read these

  • The evidence lines describe the need. They are not results of Nafs-Aql deployments, and no deployment is claimed.
  • Sources are named by organisation and year. Some figures reached us through press summaries of the primary report and are worded "as reported". One is a vendor survey and is marked so. The research note lists them.
  • Names of regulations are context for the need, not legal advice. Local processing helps with a duty of confidentiality. It is not, by itself, compliance with any regulation.
  • Rooms separate conversations, not access. There are no accounts: anyone who can reach the service can read any room. A room per client or matter organises the work; it does not restrict who can read it. Run the service with access keys when that matters.

What small models are adequate for

The models here have 1.7 and 4 billion parameters. They are adequate for answers grounded in your own documents, short drafting, summaries of a few pages, extraction, and code or maths that a checker can verify.

They are not adequate without a person's review for open-ended legal or medical reasoning, synthesis across many long documents, large changes across many files, numerical reasoning with no checker, or low-resource languages.

Regulated professions

A duty of confidentiality, and answers that must point at their source.

01

Small law firm

Find and cite clauses, dates and facts in a matter file.

Why local, CPU, private
Client confidentiality. ABA Formal Opinion 512 (2024) and the GDPR are the context.
What serves it
A knowledge base per matter with cited answers, a room per matter, and memory that can be read and deleted.
Evidence of the need
Over 80 % of firms name confidentiality and accuracy as top concerns. (ILTA, 2025, as reported by Law360)
Limit
Not legal research. Commercial legal tools were wrong on more than 17 % of queries (Stanford, 2025).
02

Healthcare administration

Answer staff questions from policies, payer rules and procedures.

Why local, CPU, private
Health information: HIPAA, and Article 9 of the GDPR.
What serves it
Answers from the documents with numbered sources. When nothing in the library matches well, the answer says so.
Evidence of the need
The average cost of a breach in healthcare was USD 7.42 million. (IBM, 2025)
Limit
Not diagnosis, triage or treatment.
03

Finance and internal audit

Check a calculation. Query the control library.

Why local, CPU, private
Bank secrecy, SOX and DORA.
What serves it
An exact arithmetic check on a calculation, and cited lookup of policies and controls.
Evidence of the need
Nearly half of privacy professionals admit entering non-public data into public tools. (Cisco, 2025)
Limit
Not judgement on accounting treatment, and not advice.
04

Tax or accounting practice

Answer questions from client folders.

Why local, CPU, private
Client information: IRC §7216 and the FTC Safeguards Rule.
What serves it
A room per client, and answers that cite the passages they rest on.
Evidence of the need
60 % of tax professionals use AI weekly. (Blue J / CPA.com, 2026, a trade-press figure)
Limit
Works only on the documents loaded.
05

Human resources

Answer staff questions from the handbook.

Why local, CPU, private
Employee data.
What serves it
Policy answers with citations.
Evidence of the need
46 % had entered employee information into public tools. (Cisco, 2025, as reported in the press)
Limit
Not hiring or dismissal decisions.
06

Compliance officer

Offer an approved alternative to unapproved chatbots.

Why local, CPU, private
Exposure from "shadow AI": tools in use that nobody approved.
What serves it
A local deployment, and memory that can be inspected.
Evidence of the need
One in five organisations reported a breach linked to shadow AI. (IBM, 2025)
Limit
Staff will still want larger models for hard tasks.

Public sector, education and research

Rules on where data may go, students' data, embargoed work and protected sources.

07

Public office

Answer staff questions from regulations and circulars.

Why local, CPU, private
Sovereignty and residency rules. Article 4 of the EU AI Act.
What serves it
A knowledge base and a graph of what the documents name, with no cloud contract.
Evidence of the need
Skills and data access are the most widespread obstacles. (OECD, 2025)
Limit
Not decisions about citizens.
08

School or university

Maths and programming practice with checked answers.

Why local, CPU, private
Student data (FERPA, GDPR), and no subscription.
What serves it
Maths checks, code run against tests in a sandbox, and course material as a knowledge base.
Evidence of the need
92 % of UK undergraduates use AI; 36 % had institutional support. (HEPI, 2025)
Limit
Prose explanations are unchecked.
09

Research lab

Work with unpublished manuscripts and a paper corpus.

Why local, CPU, private
Embargoes. NIH notice NOT-OD-23-149.
What serves it
A graph over a corpus, and cited answers.
Evidence of the need
69 % of researchers use AI tools. (Springer Nature, 2025)
Limit
Not novel scientific judgement.
10

Journalist

Connect names and dates across sensitive documents.

Why local, CPU, private
Source protection.
What serves it
Graph, search and cited passages, offline.
Evidence of the need
Over 80 % of surveyed journalists in the Global South use AI; 13 % have a policy. (Thomson Reuters Foundation, 2025)
Limit
A lead-finding aid, not evidence.

Industry and engineering

Knowledge that sits behind a plant network, in a private repository, or in a small firm's filing cabinet.

11

Manufacturing and field service

Read manuals and procedures at the machine.

Why local, CPU, private
Isolated plant networks, and places with no signal.
What serves it
An offline knowledge base whose answers carry page references.
Evidence of the need
Up to 3.8 million manufacturing workers needed by 2033 in the US. (Deloitte and The Manufacturing Institute, 2024)
Limit
Safety steps are read from the cited source. Text only.
12

Software team

Explain private code. Write and run small functions.

Why local, CPU, private
Source code is intellectual property, and there is no per-token cost.
What serves it
The code agent with sandboxed tests, and rooms.
Evidence of the need
46 % of developers do not trust AI output. (Stack Overflow, 2025)
Limit
Not large changes across files.
13

Small business

Ask about contracts, price lists and the handbook.

Why local, CPU, private
No budget for GPUs or subscriptions.
What serves it
Runs on an existing PC. The measured reference machine is a 4-core Intel i3-8100 with 32 GB.
Evidence of the need
17 % of small EU enterprises used AI in 2025, against 55 % of large ones. (Eurostat)
Limit
Slow for long outputs.

Constrained environments

Where the network is absent by rule, by law or by circumstance.

14

Air-gapped environment

Run an assistant with no outbound network at all.

Why local, CPU, private
No outbound network at all.
What serves it
No GPU, no key, and no network after installation.
Evidence of the need
91 % would choose on-premises or private infrastructure for sensitive data. (Cloudian, 2026, a vendor survey)
Limit
Updates need a manual transfer. No certification is claimed.
15

Organisation under a data-residency law

Keep processing inside the jurisdiction.

Why local, CPU, private
Local-storage and transfer rules.
What serves it
Nothing leaves the machine.
Evidence of the need
Close to 100 localisation measures in 40 countries. (OECD, 2023)
Limit
Lawful basis and retention are still the organisation's.
16

Low-connectivity setting

Use an assistant where the internet is not reliable.

Why local, CPU, private
No reliable internet.
What serves it
Fully offline once installed.
Evidence of the need
2.2 billion people offline. (ITU, 2025)
Limit
Weaker in low-resource languages.
